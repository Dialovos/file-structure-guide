# Attention residue is a tax on context switching

When you switch from task A to task B, a portion of your attention
remains on A. Sophie Leroy's 2009 study coined the term *attention
residue* and showed that the residue is largest when A was left
unfinished and time-pressured.

The residue tax is invisible at the moment of switching — task B feels
like it's starting normally. The cost shows up in slower performance
on B for the first 5-15 minutes. Multiplied over a day of frequent
switching, the tax becomes the dominant cost of fragmented work.

The practical implication: closing a task before switching (even
briefly capturing "what's next on A") cuts residue significantly,
because uncertainty about A's next step is what keeps attention
attached.

## Sources

- Leroy, S. (2009). "Why is it so hard to do my work?" *Organizational
  Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 109(2).
- Cal Newport, *Deep Work* (2016) — popularization.
